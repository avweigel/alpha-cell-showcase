Alpha Cell — membrane landscapes

Cell membranes reconstructed from isotropic FIB-SEM volumes of mouse tissue (4–8 nm voxels), from the Janelia CellMap / OpenOrganelle collection. Every surface below is real segmented data, not an illustration: each patch is a piece of a cell's boundary, one to a few micrometres across, colored by a quantitative property of the surface. Drag the 3D models to explore them.

Touch the data

These are live 3D meshes — drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan. Color encodes a measurement made at every point on the membrane.

Curvatureconvex concave  ·  Contact gap — distance to the neighboring cell across the extracellular space  ·  Deviation — protrusions and indentations relative to the smoothed surface

A sense of scale

Each patch above spans roughly 1–7 micrometres — a red blood cell is about 8 µm wide, so most of these surfaces would fit inside one. The imaging resolves detail down to 4–8 nm voxels, about the thickness of the cell membrane itself. That span — from whole-tissue context down to the lipid bilayer — is what makes this kind of volume EM data special.
